About

      

        

          
I’m a Mathematics student at UT Austin with minors in Statistics and Data Science, graduating in December 2026. I’m applying to graduate programs in quantitative finance.

          
Most of my work comes back to one question: how far can a model’s output be trusted? A backtest, a risk estimate and a forecast each carry error. I want to know where that error comes from and how large it is before anyone acts on the number.

          
In summer 2026 I was a quantitative research intern at Guangzhou Futures. I built a factor research platform covering 54 commodity futures, with signal formation and execution timestamped separately so a backtest cannot use information from the future. I also designed a net-value risk monitor for the desk’s accounts. It reads the slope of an account’s equity curve and turns it into graded de-risking alerts, and each alert rule was calibrated against the drawdowns that followed its own signals.

          
Before that I worked on data quality at China Everbright Securities and Guangdong Bozhong, checking and reconciling financial records before anyone modelled them. I still start every project by checking the data.

          
The projects on this site ask the same question of public data. The SPY/IEF atlas measures when stock–bond diversification weakened and reports the uncertainty around each estimate. The Poker Risk Lab takes one simulated risk number and separates its error into simulation noise, an uncertain win rate and the choice of model. Each page says what the analysis cannot show.

          
Away from a screen I dance Latin, skate, ride, and shoot archery. I also play tournament poker. In a PokerStars Main Event in May 2026 I finished tied for 27th of 20,210 entrants, and the Poker Risk Lab grew out of that. Earlier, I competed in DECA and finished in the top 1% at its international conference.

          
My goal is to work as a quantitative researcher. I’m looking for graduate training in probability, statistics and financial modelling to do that work rigorously.
